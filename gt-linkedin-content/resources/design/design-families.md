# Design Family Catalog

One consistent way to name what a B2B LinkedIn graphic is. Name the pattern and its family. If a graphic clearly combines two, name it `Primary + Secondary` (secondary only if it takes about 20% or more of the canvas); three or more fused is a `Composite`, listed by dominance.

## Easily confused

- **Workflow vs Blueprint:** strict arrow sequence vs numbered phases readable in any order.
- **Comparison table vs 2x2 matrix:** discrete attribute columns vs a continuous two-axis plot.
- **Stage funnel vs Workflow:** narrowing/attrition vs equal-weight steps.
- **Flywheel vs Cycle:** compounding loop vs plain repeating loop.
- **Cheat sheet vs Numbered list:** dense multi-block canvas vs one idea per line.
- **Pyramid vs Funnel:** hierarchy of levels vs downward flow.
- **Venn vs 2x2 matrix:** circles with a labeled overlap is a Venn, even if captioned "matrix".

## Design brief templates

Once you have named the family, hand the graphic off with one of these briefs. Fill the brackets, delete what does not apply, and keep the family name so the look stays consistent with past posts. The `design-briefer` sub-skill turns a finished post into one of these automatically.

### Single graphic brief (infographic, stat card, comparison, cheat sheet)

```
# Graphic brief: [working title]
Family: [from the catalog above, e.g. Category Stack, Stat Reel]
Canvas: [portrait 4:5, 1080x1350 for max feed height / square 1:1, 1080x1080]

## Top zone (the second hook)
Headline: [short, bold, readable at thumbnail size]
Support line: [one lighter line of context]
Brand: [name + handle, top corner, small]

## Body blocks
Block 1 - [label]: [what it says; include the real numbers, tool names, or steps]
Block 2 - [label]: [same]
Block 3 - [label]: [same]
(one idea per block, do not crowd)

## Visual kit
Logos: [tools or brands to show]
Icons: [what each block needs]
Connectors: [arrows, lines, or none; describe the flow]

## Emphasis
Biggest element: [the one number or phrase that should dominate]
Hierarchy: [what is second, what is small print]
Notes: [anything the designer must not miss]
```

### Carousel brief (multi-slide, one idea per slide)

```
# Carousel brief: [working title]
Slides: [8 to 12 is the sweet spot]
Canvas: portrait 4:5, 1080x1350 per slide

## Slide 1 - cover (must stop the scroll on its own)
Hook: [the boldest line, treat it as a headline]
Support: [one line under it]
Cue: [a swipe prompt at the bottom edge]

## Slide 2 - [label]
Layout: [full-bleed / split / stat-led]
Content: [the single point of this slide]
Highlight: [the number or phrase to enlarge]

## Slides 3 to N
[repeat the slide-2 pattern, one idea each, consistent styling]

## Final slide - the ask
Copy: [the CTA, matched to funnel stage]
Elements: [headshot, logo, handle, next step]
```

### Motion graphic notes (animated infographic or GIF)

- Render at 1.5x to 2x speed; feed autoplay rewards a quick payoff.
- Loop clean: the last frame should land back on the first.
- Ship silent. A looping GIF usually beats a video when there is no sound.
- Keep it under roughly 15 seconds so it can loop more than once in-feed.
- Put the headline in frame one; it does the same job as a written hook.

## Match the format to the content

| Content | Best format | Why |
|---|---|---|
| System or stack architecture | Single portrait graphic | Dense, everything visible in one frame |
| Step-by-step or multi-point | Carousel | Slides carry a natural sequence |
| One killer number or claim | Stat card or motion graphic | Movement and scale earn the stop |
| A vs B, before vs after | Comparison graphic | Side-by-side reads instantly |
| Personal story or hot take | Text-only | Let the words carry it, skip the design |

## Build rules that travel across families

**Carousels**
- 8 to 12 slides tends to hold; fewer feels thin, more gets abandoned.
- The cover is a second hook; it has to earn the swipe by itself.
- Mobile-first type: nothing smaller than about 16pt.
- One idea per slide, always.
- Keep one design language across every slide.
- One ask on the last slide (follow, comment, profile).

**Single graphics**
- The headline is a hook; make it legible at thumbnail size.
- Size the key number larger than anything else on the canvas.
- Break content into cards or panels so the eye can rest.
- Show logos for instant recognition of the tools named.
- Use arrows only when there is a real sequence to show.
