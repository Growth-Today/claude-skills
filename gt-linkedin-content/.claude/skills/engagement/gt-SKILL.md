# LinkedIn Engagement Strategy

You handle the organic side of reach: how people interact with a post, how your own commenting compounds visibility, and how to grow without setting off the platform's abuse detection. ## What the algorithm counts most

Ranked by how much each interaction moves a post, relative to a like:

- A **save** is the loudest signal there is, roughly five likes' worth, because it means someone plans to return.
- A **real comment** (fifteen words and up) is about four likes' worth and starts to build a relationship.
- A **share with a note** spreads the post into a new network, three to four likes' worth.
- A **quick comment** still counts, maybe double a like, but it says less.
- A **like** is the baseline.
- A **"see more" tap** is a soft flicker of interest, worth under a like.

## Your own commenting is a growth lever

Commenting is the highest-return activity on the platform, because it borrows other people's audiences:

- Make each comment substantial, fifteen words or more, carrying a real point, an experience, or a question.
- Leave somewhere between ten and twenty of them a day on posts in your space.
- Aim at accounts larger than yours, where the visibility payoff is bigger.
- Get in early. The first half hour after a post lands is where the top comment slot is won.
- Shape it well: a strong opening line, a break, and a question to invite a reply.

### Comment openers you can adapt

- **Build on it:** "Same thing showed up for us at [company], where [specific detail]. Have you run into [related angle]?"
- **Bring receipts:** "We ran this last quarter and landed [specific outcome]. The piece I would add is [insight]."
- **Push back politely:** "Not what I have seen, honestly. In [context] it went the other way because [evidence]. Where do you think the difference is?"

## Time-on-post

How long a reader stays with your post feeds its score even when they do not click anything. Ten seconds of reading registers as positive, an instant scroll-by as negative. Long, well-built posts and dense single-image infographics earn that extra time, so write things that reward a slow read: stories, walk-throughs, step-by-steps.

## Do not run pods

For the last couple of years LinkedIn has been catching engagement pods and quietly throttling them, because the pattern reads as artificial. The version that lasts is a genuine circle of ten to twenty peers who actually engage with each other's work. Formal pods carry rising risk; organic mutual support does not.

## Examples

**Plenty of likes, almost no comments.** The posts are likeable but not answerable. Close each one with a pointed, specific question, write things people can disagree with or reply to with their own story, and use the occasional poll to get the audience in the habit of interacting.

**A founder chasing inbound pipeline.** Spend fifteen to twenty minutes a day commenting on the posts of ICP prospects. For the DM side, lean on the profile-viewer follow-up and the caps in social-selling-campaigns.md. Never open with a pitch; ask about their problem first.

**An account restricted after pushing outreach too hard.** Pause all outbound for two to three days, then restart on the warm-up ramp in social-selling-campaigns.md. Until the limits reset, lean on organic comments instead of DMs.
