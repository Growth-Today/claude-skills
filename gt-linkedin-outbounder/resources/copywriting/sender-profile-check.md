# Sender Profile Check (before a campaign goes live)

LinkedIn weighs how closely a sender's profile matches the people it messages. A mismatch quietly suppresses reach, a match lifts acceptance. Run this on every sender before the campaign turns on. The headline is what shows next to your name when the request lands, so fix that first.

## The 60-second check

| Element | Good for outbound | Fix if... |
|---|---|---|
| Headline | Names the problem you solve for THIS audience, in their words | It reads as a job title ("Account Executive") or is generic |
| About, first line | Opens with their problem, not your career history | It starts with "I am a..." or a resume line |
| Photo | Real, recent, human face | Logo, avatar, or empty |
| Banner | One line of what you do and who it is for | Default blue, or a product screenshot |
| Recent activity | 2 to 3 relevant posts or comments in the last two weeks | Empty feed (reads as dormant or a bot) |
| Country | Matches the audience's country | US prospects messaged from a UK profile (acceptance drops) |

## Headline formula (outbound)

"I help [this audience] [result]" beats any title. Match the words to the campaign's audience.

- Good: "Helping B2B sales teams fix outbound pipeline"
- Weak: "Account Executive" or "Senior AE, EMEA"

Avoid: visionary, guru, ninja, thought leader, any title on its own.

## About, first line

The first two lines show before "see more", so treat them like a hook and lead with the reader's problem.

- Good: "Everyone wants pipeline they can predict. Here is how we build it."
- Weak: "Seasoned sales professional with 10+ years of experience..."

## Why it matters for outbound

- Match lifts acceptance, mismatch throttles reach with no warning.
- LinkedIn compares the sender's topic to the audience's world, so a sender messaging outside their apparent niche gets seen by fewer people.
- This is a pre-launch gate, not a one-time task: re-check when a sender picks up a new campaign in a different market.
